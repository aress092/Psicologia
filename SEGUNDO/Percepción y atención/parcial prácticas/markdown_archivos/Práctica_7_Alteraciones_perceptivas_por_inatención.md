> **Notas del orador:**
> - Mack & Rock (1998, p. 6): The inattention paradigm
> - The observer would neither be expecting nor looking for the object of interest, but would be looking in the general area in which it was to be presented. Subject's attention is engaged with another task, because without some distraction task, it seemed possible that by default attention might settle on the only object present.
> - Observers were asked to report the longer arm of a cross briefly presented on the screen of a computer (distraction task). One of the arms of the cross was horizontal and the other was vertical. The cross dimensions changed from trial to trial. The cross was presented on the screen of a computer for 200 msec, which is less time than it generally takes to make a saccadic eye movement. Thus we could be reasonably certain that the observers were not changing their fixation during the time the cross was visible.
> - As soon as the cross disappeared, a pattern mask appeared for 1500ms to eliminate any processing of the prior visual display. When the mask disappeared, subjects reported which line of the cross seemed longer. This procedure was followed on the first 2-3 trials. On the 3-4 trial, a critical stimulus (e.g., square) was presented in a quadrant of the cross. It was presented to subjects quite close to fixation (or at fixation) while their attention was engaged by the cross task and they were neither searching or nor expecting it.
> - Immediately following the trial in which the critical stimulus was presented, subjects were asked whether they had seen anything on the screen other than the cross figure, that is, anything that had not been present on previous trials. This question was asked throughout the research, even though it had the potential of increasing the likelihood of positive responses when nothing else had actually been seen. The answer to this question provides the data about what is perceived without attention. If the subjects reported seeing something, they were asked to identify it either by describing it or by selecting it from a set of alternatives presented to them in a recognition test.

## Diapositiva 4

- Ceguera por inatención (Inattentional blindness)

> **Notas del orador:**
> - The Invisible Gorilla Strikes Again: Sustained Inattentional Blindness in Expert Observers (2013)
> - Demonstrations of inattentional blindness have typically involved naive observers engaged in an unfamiliar task. What about expert searchers who have spent years honing their ability to detect small abnormalities in specific types of images?
> - We asked 24 radiologists to perform a familiar lung-nodule detection task. A gorilla, 48 times the size of the average nodule, was inserted in the last case that was presented. Eighty-three percent of the radiologists did not see the gorilla. Eye tracking revealed that the majority of those who missed the gorilla looked directly at its location. Thus, even expert searchers, operating in their domain of expertise, are vulnerable to inattentional blindness.
> - Fig. 2:  a) computed-tomography image containing the embedded gorilla, and b) and eye-position plot of a radiologist who did not report seeing the gorilla. In b) the blue circles represent eye positions recorded at 1-ms intervals.
> - Graph: The graph shows the rate of inattentional blindness (i.e., the percentage of observers who did not report seeing the gorilla) among the radiologists and the naive observers.

> **Notas del orador:**
> - El parpadeo de la atención (Beltrán, 2010, Ciencia Cognitiva)
> - La mayoría de nosotros sabe que los ojos parpadean espontáneamente. Muchos tenemos también la certeza de que este movimiento se produce varias veces por segundo. Pero lo que pocos conocen es que nuestro sistema atencional también parpadea. Pero así es. Aunque sea en un sentido metafórico, nuestra capacidad para mantener la atención sobre series de estímulos visuales sufre de apagones espontáneos que bloquean la identificación de algunos estímulos. Según recientes estudios, tanto el entrenamiento en técnicas de meditación como la presencia de estímulos afectivos pueden modular la ocurrencia de estos apagones a los que los investigadores llaman parpadeos de la atención.

## Diapositiva 13

- Shapiro y cols. (1997)
- Características del parpadeo atencional
- Se necesita atención para percibir el primer estímulo (T1), por lo que hay menos recursos atencionales disponibles para el segundo estímulo (T2).
- Se produce cierto grado de procesamiento pero no el suficiente para que T2 sea percibido de forma consciente y poder informar de él.
- Si dejamos pasar el suficiente tiempo para que T1 sea procesado (aprox. 500ms), T2 se percibe.
